Blue Panda registration workflow

  1. Open the Virginia FBLA conference registration site and log in through the FBLA Connect option.
  2. Select the correct Virginia FBLA State Leadership Conference registration.
  3. Add attendees. For members, select the student from the available member list rather than manually creating a student.
  4. Enter required information such as special needs and food restrictions, then save.
  5. Open Competitions and assign the appropriate student(s) to each event. Double-check team composition.
  6. Use the Issues area before submission. Resolve anything that blocks submission.
  7. Submit by the current Virginia deadline. Save/print confirmation according to the current guide.
Adviser habit: Have a second adult/student officer cross-check attendee names, events and team members before final submission.

The 3 competition formats

Middle School and High School events fit these three common formats. Knowing which one a student is entering changes how you train them.

🎭 Roleplays

What it is: Students get a business scenario and a short prep window, then act it out in front of a judge.

  • Practice with unfamiliar scenarios — not just the one they rehearsed.
  • Drill the opening: state the problem and your plan in the first 30 seconds.
  • Time-box prep and delivery so pacing feels natural under pressure.
  • Use peers as mock judges with the real rating sheet.

🎤 Presentations

What it is: Students prepare a talk, pitch or project and present it to judges, often with visuals.

  • Build the deck early, then spend reps on delivery, not slides.
  • Practice Q&A — judges probe beyond the prepared script.
  • Record a practice run and review one or two specific fixes.
  • Check the official guidelines for time limits, materials and judging criteria.

📝 Objective tests

What it is: A scored test of business knowledge — the format that rewards consistent study most.

  • Take timed practice tests to build pacing and reduce test anxiety.
  • Review wrong answers and turn them into a short study list.
  • Use the current year's topic outline as the study map.
  • Schedule short, repeated study blocks rather than one cram session.

Match the student to the format

Comfortable speaking and thinking on their feet? Try a roleplay. Strong at building and delivering a polished message? A presentation. Best at memorizing and recalling facts under time pressure? An objective test. Let students sample all three early so they choose by strength, not by what a friend picked.

Funding: separate the buckets

Teach advisers to build a funding stack

Don't treat “pay for States” as one giant problem. Break costs into categories, then identify the appropriate school/district and community funding sources.

Chapter events

Local meetings, activities, speakers, small trips.

State travel

Conference registration, transportation, lodging/meals where applicable.

Fundraisers

Use approved school fundraising procedures and start early.

CTE / Perkins

Ask your school's CTE administration what allowable student-organization expenses can be supported locally.

HEF / education funds

Check district/school eligibility and current rules.

Sponsors

Local businesses/community partners can sometimes support student participation.

Don't promise funding. Funding eligibility, account codes, reimbursement rules and approval processes are local. Advisers should verify with their school/district before collecting or spending money.

Make students want to stay

FBLA should be more than competition

🏢 Business field trips

Visit a local business, bank, startup, nonprofit, media company or government office. End with a student reflection: “What skill did I see in action?”

🎙️ Speaker series

Invite alumni, entrepreneurs, executives, college students and community leaders. Ask them to bring one real problem for students to solve.

💼 Career challenge

Run a mini case competition, Shark Tank-style pitch, budgeting challenge or marketing sprint using age-appropriate scenarios.

🤝 Chapter social

Use low-cost team-building so new members develop friendships and feel ownership of the chapter.

🏆 Recognition

Celebrate improvement, practice consistency, leadership and service—not only winners.

🌎 Service

Connect community service to leadership development and let students plan and execute a project.
